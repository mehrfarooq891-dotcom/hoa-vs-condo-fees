HOA Fees vs Condo Fees: The Difference Is the Building

Listings use the terms interchangeably, but the coverage is not the same. Both are mandatory recurring dues paid to a community association. The difference is what the association is responsible for maintaining — and that difference is why condo fees run higher.

HOA fees: the neighbourhood

A single-family HOA fee funds shared neighbourhood grounds: private roads, entrance landscaping, streetlights, parks and recreational amenities such as a pool or clubhouse. The homeowner remains individually responsible for their own roof, siding, windows and home insurance.

Condo fees: the neighbourhood plus the building

A condo fee covers the same shared amenities plus the structural envelope of the building: exterior walls, flat roofs, foundations, hallways, elevators, master hazard insurance, shared utilities and outer building maintenance. Because the association must fund multi-million-dollar structural reserve accounts, HOAFeeCalculator.com notes condo dues average 40% to 150% higher than single-family subdivision dues.

The master-association wrinkle

Some homes sit inside layered planned communities and pay twice: a neighbourhood sub-association fee plus a master association fee for regional parks and shared facilities. HOAFeeCalculator.com's guide gives $150 a month to a sub-association plus $75 to a master association as a typical example. Always ask for both bills before you buy — the listing usually shows only one.
